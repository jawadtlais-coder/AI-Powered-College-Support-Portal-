# LIU Registration Quick Guide

Source notes:
- Use this guide for general Lebanese International University registration, admission, transfer, and student-support answers.
- Public LIU pages and catalog material can change. Exact deadlines, forms, payment amounts, NSSF clearance status, and personal account decisions must be verified with the relevant LIU campus office, Admission and Registration, or Student Affairs.
- If a student asks about their own record, payment, missing documents, section availability, hold, NSSF clearance, or transfer-credit decision, the assistant should answer generally and recommend creating a registration support ticket.

## General Admission and Registration

Q: What does the Admission and Registration Office help with?
A: Admission and Registration helps with enrollment applications, admission requirements, registration guidelines, student records, and registration processes connected to LIU and Ministry of Education and Higher Education requirements.

Q: Where can I apply to LIU?
A: Students can usually apply online through LIU systems or apply directly through an admission office with help from admission staff.

Q: What happens after I submit an online application?
A: The student should complete the admission file by submitting the required official documents to the admission office. Applications and official documents submitted to LIU are treated as LIU records and are handled by the admission office.

Q: If I get accepted and do not register this year, what happens?
A: Admission is usually valid for the current academic year. If you do not register, you may need to apply again or confirm your admission status with Admission and Registration.

Q: Can the assistant confirm if I am accepted?
A: No. Acceptance depends on the student's file and admission office review. The assistant can explain the general process, but the student must contact Admission and Registration or create a ticket for personal admission status.

Q: Can the assistant register courses for me?
A: No. The assistant cannot perform registration actions or change a student's schedule. It can explain general steps and help create a support ticket if registration is blocked.

Q: What information should I include when asking for registration help?
A: Include your LIU ID, campus, program or major, academic department, the registration term, the course or section if relevant, any error message, and a clear description of what you need.

## Common Registration Problems

Q: Why might I be blocked from registration?
A: Common reasons include missing admission documents, unpaid or pending fees, NSSF or social security clearance, academic advising requirements, department approval, prerequisite problems, closed sections, schedule conflicts, or a hold on the student's account.

Q: What should I do if I have a registration hold?
A: Contact Admission and Registration, Student Affairs, or your campus office with your LIU ID and the exact hold message. If using this portal, create a registration ticket and attach a screenshot if possible.

Q: Why do I have a registration hold at LIU?
A: A registration hold may be related to missing documents, unpaid or pending fees, NSSF or social security clearance, academic advising, department approval, prerequisite issues, or another account requirement. The student must check the exact hold with the campus office or create a registration ticket.

Q: What should I do if the system says documents are missing?
A: Ask Admission and Registration which document is missing, whether it must be certified, and where it should be submitted. For a support ticket, include your LIU ID, campus, program, and the name of the missing document if known.

Q: What should I do if a course section is full?
A: Contact your academic department or campus registration office. The assistant can explain the issue, but only the department or registration office can confirm whether another section, override, or waitlist option is available.

Q: What should I do if I cannot register because of a prerequisite?
A: Contact your department or advisor. If you believe the prerequisite was already completed or transferred, create a registration ticket with the course code, previous course information, and any transcript or transfer decision if available.

Q: What should I do if I selected the wrong course or section?
A: Contact Registration or your academic advisor as soon as possible. Course add/drop rules and deadlines are campus and term specific, so the assistant should not promise that a change is allowed.

Q: Can I register if my academic department is not assigned in the portal?
A: No. The portal needs your academic department for ticket routing. Contact an admin or create a support request asking for your academic department to be assigned.

## Documents

Q: What documents are commonly needed for new undergraduate admission?
A: Requirements vary by case, but students are commonly asked for official school credentials such as Lebanese Baccalaureate, Technical Baccalaureate, or recognized equivalent, identification documents, photos, and any documents requested by Admission and Registration.

Q: What documents are commonly requested from transfer students?
A: Transfer students are commonly asked for a certified copy of the Lebanese Baccalaureate, Technical Baccalaureate, or recognized equivalent; certified university transcript; course descriptions; identification card copy; family civil status record copy; recent passport-size colored photo; and any social security or NSSF papers required for Lebanese applicants.

Q: What documents are needed for LIU transfer students?
A: LIU transfer students commonly need a certified Lebanese Baccalaureate, Technical Baccalaureate, or recognized equivalent; certified university transcript; course descriptions; ID copy; family civil status record copy; recent passport-size colored photo; and any required NSSF or social security papers for Lebanese applicants. The student should confirm the final list with Admission and Registration.

Q: What papers do I need to transfer to LIU?
A: For transfer to LIU, prepare certified school equivalence or Baccalaureate documents, certified university transcript, course descriptions, identification card copy, family civil status record copy, recent passport-size photo, and any required NSSF documents. Transfer-credit approval still depends on school or department review.

Q: Do documents need to be certified?
A: Many admission and transfer documents must be certified or official. Students should confirm certification requirements with Admission and Registration before submitting.

Q: Can I submit screenshots instead of official documents?
A: Screenshots may help explain a support issue, but official admission and transfer decisions usually require official or certified documents. The student should verify document format with Admission and Registration.

Q: What if my foreign certificate needs equivalence?
A: Foreign certificates may need official equivalence or recognition according to Lebanese Ministry and LIU requirements. The student should confirm the exact equivalence process with Admission and Registration.

## Transfer Students and Credit Transfer

Q: Does LIU accept transfer students?
A: Yes. LIU accepts transfer students from recognized institutions. Transfer transcripts are reviewed to decide which courses may count toward the student's LIU program.

Q: Does LIU automatically accept all transfer credits?
A: No. Transfer credits are reviewed by the relevant school or department. Courses must fit the LIU program requirements and meet the applicable transfer criteria.

Q: Do transfer credits count in my LIU GPA?
A: Accepted transfer credits generally do not count toward the LIU GPA. They may count toward degree requirements if approved.

Q: How much of my degree must be completed at LIU?
A: Transfer rules usually require students to complete at least 51% of undergraduate degree credits at LIU. Some graduate transfer limits may be stricter and depend on school rules.

Q: Can a department refuse transfer credit for a major course?
A: Yes. A department may refuse transfer credit for major requirements, even if a similar course was taken elsewhere, if it needs to ensure the required learning outcomes are met.

Q: What should I include in a transfer-credit support ticket?
A: Include your LIU ID, previous university name, program, LIU program, course code and title, transcript, course description or syllabus, and the specific course you want evaluated.

Q: Can the assistant tell me exactly how many credits will transfer?
A: No. Exact transfer-credit decisions require official review by the school or department. The assistant can explain the general policy and help prepare a ticket.

## English Language and Placement

Q: Does LIU teach in English?
A: Yes. LIU uses English as the main language of instruction and communication for courses.

Q: Do I need English proficiency?
A: Students may need to show English proficiency through accepted tests or complete LIU English placement requirements. The required level depends on the program and admission decision.

Q: What is the English placement test?
A: The English placement process evaluates a student's English level. Depending on the result, LIU may require English or remedial courses before or during the student's program.

Q: Can the assistant waive my English placement test?
A: No. Only LIU Admission and Registration or the relevant academic office can confirm exemptions, placement, or required English courses.

## Fees, Tuition, and Financial Aid

Q: How are LIU tuition fees commonly calculated?
A: LIU tuition is commonly based on the number of registered credits. Other costs may be separate, such as registration fees, books, social security or NSSF fees, entrance exams, and other administrative charges.

Q: How much does LIU registration cost?
A: The assistant should not give a final registration cost because amounts can change by year, campus, program, credit load, and student status. LIU tuition is commonly credit based, and extra charges such as registration, books, English entrance exam, or NSSF/social security fees may apply. The student should verify current fees with LIU.

Q: Can the assistant give exact tuition or fee amounts?
A: The assistant should avoid promising exact current amounts because fees can change by year, program, campus, and student status. The student should verify current fees with LIU.

Q: Where can I ask about financial aid?
A: Students who need financial assistance should ask the relevant LIU office for the financial aid application process. Student Affairs and campus offices can guide students to the correct form or office.

Q: Can I register if my payment is still pending?
A: Payment status is personal account information. The assistant cannot verify it. Contact the cashier, finance office, Registration, or create a support ticket with your LIU ID and payment details.

## NSSF and Social Security

Q: What is NSSF clearance?
A: LIU Student Affairs describes the Social Security Unit as the liaison between students and the National Social Security Fund. NSSF clearance may be required before registration at the beginning of the academic year.

Q: Why do I need NSSF clearance before registration?
A: NSSF clearance may be required because LIU Student Affairs handles social security processes with the National Social Security Fund. Public LIU information says clearance can be a prerequisite before registration at the beginning of the academic year.

Q: Who may need NSSF or social security papers?
A: Lebanese students may be required to submit national social security papers or complete the required process, depending on government and LIU rules.

Q: Who may be excluded from NSSF membership?
A: Public LIU Student Affairs information says NSSF membership requirements exclude freshman and special students and students older than 30. Students should still verify their case with Student Affairs.

Q: What if I already have NSSF or equivalent coverage?
A: Students who are already affiliated with NSSF or an equivalent fund may need to submit an exemption form through the proper LIU/NSSF process.

Q: Can the assistant confirm my NSSF clearance?
A: No. NSSF clearance is personal status information. The student must contact Student Affairs, the Social Security Unit, or create a support ticket.

## Student Affairs and Support

Q: What can Student Affairs help with?
A: Student Affairs supports financial aid guidance, social security and NSSF processes, student activities, community service, athletics, student engagement, counseling services, educational services, complaints, grievances, and appeals.

Q: Who can help me with registration problems?
A: Student Affairs, Admission and Registration, the academic department, or the relevant campus office can help depending on the issue.

Q: What is the Student Affairs email?
A: student-affairs@liu.edu.lb

Q: What is the Student Affairs phone number?
A: Public LIU material lists Student Affairs phone contact as (+961) 1-706881/2/3/4 Ext. Students should verify campus-specific extensions with LIU.

Q: What if I have a complaint, grievance, or appeal?
A: Student Affairs handles complaints, grievances, and appeals in coordination with the relevant offices. Students should provide their LIU ID, clear facts, dates, and supporting documents.

## Graduation and Academic Requirements

Q: What is GPA?
A: GPA means Grade Point Average. It is a summary of a student's academic performance based on course grades. LIU may refer to GPA or cumulative grade when checking academic standing, graduation requirements, or program rules. Students should verify their official GPA and exact calculation with Registration, their department, or their academic advisor.

Q: How is GPA calculated?
A: GPA calculation depends on the student's program, registered courses, credits, grades, repeated courses, and current LIU academic rules. The assistant can explain that GPA summarizes academic performance, but students should verify the exact official calculation with Registration, their department, or their academic advisor.

Q: What does an undergraduate student need to graduate?
A: Undergraduate students must complete required credits and courses, meet major and general education requirements, satisfy minimum grade or GPA requirements, meet residency rules, and finish clearance procedures.

Q: What minimum grade is commonly required for undergraduate graduation?
A: Many LIU catalog rules refer to a minimum cumulative grade of 70% or 2.0 out of 4.0 for undergraduate programs, but exact rules vary by school and program.

Q: What does a graduate student need to graduate?
A: Graduate students must complete required credits, program requirements, thesis or project requirements if applicable, minimum grade or GPA requirements, residency rules, and clearance procedures.

Q: Are graduation requirements the same for all schools?
A: No. Requirements differ by school and program, such as Business, Engineering, Arts and Sciences, Education, and Pharmacy. Students should check their catalog and department.

## Ticket Escalation Rules

Q: When should the assistant create or recommend a registration ticket?
A: Create or recommend a ticket when the question depends on personal status, a hold, missing documents, payment, NSSF clearance, transfer-credit evaluation, course section availability, prerequisite override, campus-specific rules, or any deadline not present in trusted knowledge.

Q: What should a registration ticket contain?
A: A strong ticket includes LIU ID, full name, campus, academic department, program or major, term, course code if relevant, exact error or request, screenshots or files, and what office already said if the student contacted anyone.

Q: What should the assistant avoid saying?
A: The assistant should not promise admission, registration approval, fee amounts, transfer-credit approval, NSSF clearance, course override approval, graduation clearance, or deadline exceptions.

Q: What can the assistant safely say?
A: The assistant can explain general LIU registration guidance, list common documents, describe transfer and NSSF processes, help draft a clear ticket, and direct students to Admission and Registration, Student Affairs, or the relevant campus office.
